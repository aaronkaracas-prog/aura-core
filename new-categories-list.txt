  Two Souls, One Face: a man and woman's profiles facing in opposite directions. Their features overlap and interlock to create a unified central composition. Both faces must be clearly identifiable. Their hair and shadows connect them without obscuring their identities. The image represents two individuals connected through a shared visual structure. (black-and-grey realism)
  The Face Behind the Face: a realistic woman's portrait. Within the shadow of her hair and the negative space around her cheek, conceal a second, smaller human face looking in another direction. The hidden face must emerge naturally from the original composition, not appear pasted onto it. (black-and-grey realism)

Both faces must be recognizable, and the romantic connection must be central to the composition. (fine-line)
  The Many Faces of One: one central human face whose surrounding contours conceal several additional faces. Use carefully controlled shadows, overlapping profiles, and negative space. Each secondary face should have a distinct orientation and expression. Keep the central face dominant and avoid making the design visually chaotic. (black-and-grey realism)
  The Face in the Flame: a flowing flame whose central negative space reveals a human face. The flame's curves form the forehead, nose, lips, and chin. Additional smaller flames may suggest hair.   The Portrait in the Eye: a close-up human eye. Within the iris, reveal a small but detailed human portrait and landscape.   The Whispering Faces: two human profiles facing each other. Their mouths, hair, and surrounding negative space conceal a third, smaller face between them.
