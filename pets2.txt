GLOBAL PETS GENERATION PROMPT

Create exceptional contemporary tattoo inspiration centered on a specific beloved pet.

PETS = WHO I LOVE.

The emotional subject is not a generic species.

It is one individual animal.

The example animal shown in each concept is only an example.

NEVER LET THE EXAMPLE PET BECOME THE DESIGN.

THE DESIGN MUST STILL WORK WHEN THE CUSTOMER REPLACES THE EXAMPLE WITH THEIR OWN PET.

If the concept shows a Doberman, the underlying artistic idea should still work with a Golden Retriever, mixed-breed dog, cat, rabbit, horse, bird or another companion animal whenever logically appropriate.

Rotate pets continuously.

Do not create a catalog dominated by one or two popular breeds.

Include a broad natural mix of dogs, cats, horses, rabbits, birds, reptiles, small mammals and unusual companion animals.

Within dogs, rotate breeds, sizes, coat types, ages and appearances.

Within cats, rotate coat patterns, breeds, body types and ages.

Include mixed-breed animals.

Include senior animals.

Include young animals.

Include rescued animals.

Include animals with physical characteristics that make them individually recognizable.

Do not beautify away identity.

If the real dog has one ear that sits differently, preserve it.

If the cat has a torn ear, preserve it.

If the senior animal has a grey muzzle, preserve it.

If the pet has one eye, preserve it.

If the animal has an unusual tooth, scar, coat patch, blaze, feather pattern, scale pattern or posture, preserve it.

INDIVIDUALITY IS MORE IMPORTANT THAN BREED PERFECTION.

Use the full artistic vocabulary of tattooing.

Realism.

Black and Grey Realism.

Colour Realism.

Micro Realism.

Fine Line.

Sketch.

Dotwork.

Blackwork.

Watercolour.

Illustrative.

Neo-Traditional.

New School.

Geometric.

Negative Space.

Ornamental.

Surrealism.

And intelligent combinations.

Do not rotate styles mechanically.

Choose a style because it improves the specific idea.

A realistic portrait may be right when exact likeness is the emotional center.

Sketch may be right when movement or memory is important.

Watercolour may be right when softness or color carries the feeling.

Blackwork may be right for a dark animal or powerful silhouette.

Fine Line may be right for intimacy and restraint.

Dotwork may be right for texture.

New School may be right for a pet with enormous personality.

Ornamental treatment may be right when the pet is being elevated into something precious.

The style serves the relationship.

Pet tattoos do not always need the pet's face.

Explore:

Actual paw prints.

Actual nose prints.

Actual collars.

Actual tags.

Actual toys.

Actual blankets.

Actual feathers.

Actual horseshoes.

Favorite objects.

Favorite sleeping positions.

Favorite places.

Favorite walks.

Daily routines.

Car rides.

Windows.

Beds.

Chairs.

Trails.

Hands touching fur.

Forehead-to-forehead contact.

The way an animal looked back.

The way it waited.

The way it slept.

The way it ran.

The way it sat beside someone.

The ordinary behavior may contain more emotional truth than a formal portrait.

DO NOT USE GENERIC PAW PRINTS WHEN THE CONCEPT CALLS FOR THE CUSTOMER'S ACTUAL PAW PRINT.

DO NOT USE GENERIC TAGS WHEN THE CUSTOMER'S REAL TAG CAN BE USED.

DO NOT INVENT PERSONAL DETAILS.

The design should invite personalization.

Multiple pets must form one composition.

Do not default to stacking floating animal heads.

Show relationships.

Pets can sleep together.

Run together.

Walk together.

Touch noses.

Share furniture.

Occupy the same environment.

Interact naturally.

If portrait composition is used, connect the portraits through deliberate structure rather than merely arranging heads vertically.

MEMORIAL IS PART OF PETS.

MEMORIAL IS NOT ALL OF PETS.

Living pets should be celebrated just as strongly.

Joy does not require comedy.

Show companionship.

Energy.

Trust.

Age.

Habit.

Comfort.

Adventure.

Home.

Personality.

Affection.

Memorial concepts should avoid automatic clichés.

Do not automatically add:

Angel wings.

Halos.

Heavenly clouds.

Generic infinity symbols.

Generic heartbeat lines.

Generic paw icons.

RIP text.

Clocks.

Dates surrounded by decorative filler.

These elements can be used when specifically meaningful to the customer, but death itself does not require a visual symbol.

Sometimes the empty collar is enough.

Sometimes the sleeping position is enough.

Sometimes the old tennis ball is enough.

Sometimes the grey muzzle is enough.

Sometimes the familiar window is enough.

Sometimes the animal simply walking away is enough.

Design for the body.

Do not place a rectangular photograph on skin.

Allow portraits to fade naturally.

Allow full bodies to follow anatomy.

Allow horses to use long placements.

Allow birds to open across shoulders.

Allow snakes and long-bodied pets to wrap.

Allow sleeping animals to form natural curved compositions.

Allow multiple pets enough space to interact.

Use small tattoos where intimacy benefits from small scale.

Use medium tattoos for most portrait and relationship concepts.

Use large scale only when the artistic idea deserves it.

Do not make everything a portrait.

Do not make everything memorial.

Do not make everything sentimental.

Do not make everything cute.

Do not make everything realistic.

Do not make everything Fine Line.

Do not make everything a dog.

Do not make everything a cat.

Do not make everything a Golden Retriever.

Do not make everything a Maine Coon.

Do not make every animal young and perfect.

Do not use generic breed stereotypes as personality.

The customer's animal provides the personality.

THE PET IS NOT A SYMBOL UNTIL THE CUSTOMER MAKES IT ONE.

THE PET IS FIRST A RELATIONSHIP.

The goal of every inspiration image is for someone to think:

THAT IS NOT MY PET.

BUT I WANT THAT IDEA WITH MY PET.


CUSTOMER DESCRIPTION


WHAT IS IT?

Pets is tattoo inspiration built around the animal you actually love.

Your dog.

Your cat.

Your horse.

Your bird.

Your rabbit.

Your reptile.

Your small companion.

Or another animal that is part of your life.

It can be a portrait.

But it does not have to be.

The tattoo can come from their face, markings, paw print, nose print, collar, tag, favorite toy, sleeping position, favorite place or something the two of you always did together.


WHAT DOES IT LOOK LIKE?

Anything from a tiny Fine Line portrait to a major Colour Realism piece.

Pets can become:

Realism.

Black and Grey.

Fine Line.

Sketch.

Watercolour.

Dotwork.

Blackwork.

Illustrative.

Neo-Traditional.

New School.

Geometric.

Negative Space.

Ornamental.

Or something that combines several artistic approaches.

The important thing is that the animal remains recognizable as yours.


WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT?

The closest category is Animals.

But the reason for entering each category is different.

PETS = WHO I LOVE.

ANIMALS = WHAT I LOVE.

If you want a tiger because you think tigers are incredible, explore Animals.

If you want your cat because she has slept beside you for fifteen years, explore Pets.

Pets starts with a relationship.

Animals starts with fascination with the animal itself.


WHAT DOES IT WORK WELL FOR?

Dogs.

Cats.

Horses.

Birds.

Rabbits.
